# Revised category vignettes (receiver behavior removed)

Each game has exactly six vignette categories: Moral, Self-interest, and Cooperation, each in a personal and an anonymous setting. Realized receiver behavior is never stated. UG and TG cooperation variants that previously differed by receiver cooperation are collapsed into a single vignette per setting.

## DG

### DG-M-P — Moral, personal

Anna and Marco helped organize a neighborhood festival. The association later sends Anna a bundle of meal vouchers as a thank-you and tells her that she may keep them all or pass some on to Marco. Anna gives Marco half. They both put a lot of time into the event, and she feels it is fair to share the reward between them. She views this as a matter of moral principle and thinks that an equal split is the morally right thing to do in this situation.

### DG-M-A — Moral, anonymous

Firm A and Firm B complete a one-time order through an online marketplace. The platform pays a transaction bonus entirely to Firm A and allows A to transfer any part of it to Firm B. Firm A's manager splits the bonus equally. Since both firms contributed to completing the order, she views the division as a matter of fairness and moral principle. In her view, an equal split is the morally right way to divide the reward.

### DG-S-P — Self-interest, personal

David and Laura finish a demanding work assignment together. A travel agency later sends David a set of promotional travel credits and tells him that he may give some to Laura if he wants. David keeps all of them. The credits are useful to him, and giving some away would leave him with less. Since the decision is his, he prefers the option that maximizes his own payoff.

### DG-S-A — Self-interest, anonymous

Firm A and Firm B complete a one-time sale through an online marketplace. The platform assigns the entire sales rebate to Firm A but allows A to transfer any portion to Firm B. Firm A keeps the full rebate. The manager of A thinks that any transfer would reduce her firm's earnings. She therefore chooses the option that maximizes her firm's payoff.

### DG-C-P — Cooperation, personal

Giovanni and Paola work together on a freelance project. Giovanni brings in the client, while Paola provides the technical expertise needed to finish the job. The client pays the entire completion bonus to Giovanni and leaves it to him to decide whether to share it. Giovanni views the project as a win-win collaboration made possible by their complementary contributions and shares the completion bonus with Paola.

### DG-C-A — Cooperation, anonymous

Firm A and Firm B are matched through an online platform for a one-time production order. Firm A brings the customer, while Firm B supplies a component without which the order could not be completed. The platform pays the entire completion bonus to Firm A and leaves any transfer to B at A's discretion. The manager views the match as a value-creating collaboration made possible by the firms' complementary contributions and shares the completion bonus with Firm B.

## UG

### UG-M-P — Moral, personal

Elena and Paolo had both prepared for a small concert that was cancelled at the last minute. The organizer offers them a one-time compensation payment, but it will be paid only if both sign the same settlement. Elena is asked to propose how the payment should be divided. She views the decision as a matter of fairness and moral principle and suggests an equal split because both of them had put time into preparing for the concert. She thinks that equal division is the morally right thing to do.

### UG-M-A — Moral, anonymous

Two firms complete preparatory work for an order that is later cancelled. The platform offers a fixed compensation payment, but it will release the money only if both firms agree on how it is divided. Firm A is asked to propose the split. Its manager views the decision as a matter of fairness and moral principle because both firms incurred costs preparing for the order. She thinks that the morally right thing to do is an equal split and proposes it.

### UG-S-P — Self-interest, personal

Luca and Giulia are offered a one-time payment after an event they had planned together is cancelled. The organizer will pay only if both agree on the division, and Luca is asked to make the proposal. Luca wants to keep as much of the payment as possible for himself. He therefore offers Giulia only a small positive share and keeps most of the payment for himself.

### UG-S-A — Self-interest, anonymous

A retailer has a one-time order from a final customer and needs a supplier to complete it. The retailer proposes how the available margin will be divided, and the order goes through only if the supplier agrees. The retailer's manager wants to keep as much of the margin as possible for her firm. She therefore offers the supplier only a small positive share and keeps most of the margin for her firm.

### UG-C-P — Cooperation, personal

Chiara and Stefano are offered a one-time payment for a community project they developed together, but the sponsor will release the money only if they agree on its division. Chiara sees agreement as a valuable win-win opportunity for both of them. She proposes a substantial share to Stefano so that both can benefit from reaching an agreement while still leaving a good return for herself.

### UG-C-A — Cooperation, anonymous

A producer and a distributor are considering a one-time order that will create a substantial margin if they reach an agreement. The producer proposes how that margin will be divided, and the order goes through only if the distributor agrees. The producer's manager sees agreement as a valuable profit opportunity for both firms and proposes a substantial share to the distributor so that both sides can benefit from the transaction.

## TG

### TG-M-P — Moral, personal

Miriam and Roberto discover a box of collectible books being sold cheaply at a clearance sale. Miriam has the cash to buy them, while Roberto knows collectors who would pay much more for the books and has offered to handle the resale. Miriam decides to finance half of the available purchase. She views the decision as a matter of fairness and moral principle: since Roberto is also contributing to the opportunity, she thinks that giving him an equal stake is the morally right thing to do.

### TG-M-A — Moral, anonymous

A small wholesaler and an independent broker are matched through an online marketplace for a one-time resale opportunity. The wholesaler owns a batch of goods, while the broker has access to buyers willing to pay much more for them. The wholesaler consigns half of the available goods to the broker. Its manager views the decision as a matter of fairness and moral principle: since the broker is also contributing something essential, giving both sides a comparable stake is, in the manager's view, the morally right thing to do.

### TG-S-P — Self-interest, personal

Nicola has some savings, and Beatrice tells him about a batch of vintage furniture she can buy cheaply and resell through her contacts for several times the purchase price. Beatrice lacks enough cash to buy the whole batch, so Nicola can finance part of the deal. The arrangement is informal, and Beatrice would control the sale proceeds. Nicola is seriously concerned that she may keep most or all of the money once the furniture is sold. To avoid putting his own payoff at risk, he contributes only a very small amount and keeps most of his savings.

### TG-S-A — Self-interest, anonymous

A small wholesaler and an independent broker are matched through an online platform for a one-time resale opportunity. The broker has access to a buyer willing to pay several times the wholesaler's cost, but the parties have no enforceable agreement over how the resale proceeds will be divided. The wholesaler is seriously concerned that the broker may retain most or all of the proceeds once the sale is completed. To protect its own payoff from that risk, it consigns only a small quantity of goods and keeps the rest.

### TG-C-P — Cooperation, personal

Camilla has some money set aside, while Federico has found a batch of vintage records that he can buy cheaply and resell to collectors for much more. Federico does not have enough cash to purchase the whole batch, and Camilla could finance part of the purchase. Camilla views this as a valuable win-win opportunity in which their complementary contributions can create a larger joint return. She therefore provides a substantial part of the money so that the opportunity can generate gains for both of them.

### TG-C-A — Cooperation, anonymous

A small wholesaler and an independent broker are matched through an online marketplace for a one-time resale opportunity. The broker has access to buyers willing to pay several times the wholesaler's cost but needs inventory to complete the deal. The wholesaler's manager sees the match as a valuable win-win opportunity in which the two firms' complementary contributions can create a larger joint return. The wholesaler therefore consigns a substantial quantity so that the transaction can generate gains for both firms.
